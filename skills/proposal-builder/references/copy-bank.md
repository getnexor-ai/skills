# Proposal copy bank

Patterns distilled from shipped Nexor proposals. Examples use fictional brands;
swap in the real prospect. Every line here obeys the copy rules in SKILL.md
(no hyphens or dashes in visible copy, middots as separators, bold only on the
load bearing fact).

## Hero headlines

Two shapes. Pick one.

**The mirrored couplet** — two short imperative sentences, the object of the
first becomes the subject of the second. Name the prospect's raw input and
their money metric:

- `Turn sessions into conversations.<br>Turn conversations into orders.` (retail: sessions → orders)
- `Turn quote requests into conversations.<br>Turn conversations into reservations.` (rentals/travel: quotes → reservations)
- `Turn visits into conversations.<br>Turn conversations into signed leases.` (real estate)
- `Turn demo requests into conversations.<br>Turn conversations into booked meetings.` (B2B)

Optional: wrap the final money word in `<span class="gr">…</span>` to hit it
with the accent color.

**The founder promise** — for founder led or personality brands, put the
founder in the headline and make the promise absolute:

- `Every man who walks in gets Georg.<br>Even at midnight. Even in the cart.`
  → shape: `Every {customer noun} who walks in gets {founder first name}.<br>Even at {unstaffed hour}. Even in the {drop off point}.`

## The subhead

One fixed skeleton, always. It does three jobs: what it is, what it does that
nothing else does, and who is in control.

> An agent layer under {domain}. It {answers/quotes} any {customer noun} in
> seconds{, optional brand knowledge clause}, then **chases the
> {order/booking/lease} across {Channel list}**, without tiring, until it earns
> a yes or a no. You configure the voice, the rules and the limits.

- Nurture-shaped businesses (long consideration): swap the chase clause for
  "**follows the {customer noun} across {channels} until the {reservation} is
  booked**. You set the voice, the rules and the limits."
- Brand knowledge clause example (boutique): "with the fit notes and fabric
  knowledge behind every shirt".
- Channel list: US prospects `Call, iMessage, Email and Instagram`; LATAM
  prospects `Call, WhatsApp, Email and Instagram`. Call always first.

## Chat vignette (page 1 mock)

4 to 5 bubbles, always this arc: **specific product doubt → confident answer
with a reason + micro close → second doubt (logistics or durability) → answer
that removes it + states the cart is already handled → delighted close.**

The agent voice: short declaratives, gives the reason before the
recommendation ("Going by the fit notes, the **M** is the safe pick"), never
hedges, moves to the cart without asking permission twice. The customer's
questions carry concrete nouns (a named product, a real city, kids' ages).

Persona: a common human first name + "from {Company}" (`Rachel from
{RentalCo}`, `Marcus from {BoutiqueCo}`). The chat header right slot always
says `Nexor`.

## Use case cards (page 1, exactly three)

Order is fixed: **(1) inbound concierge, (2) the money recovery play,
(3) winback.** Titles are 3 to 7 words, verb or noun phrase, no colon.

Descriptions: one or two sentences. Open with the capability or the painful
stat, bold the mechanism, close with the outcome. Pills: 3 or 4, each 2 to 4
words, capability nouns ("Cart aware outreach", "Discount authority you set",
"Stops on purchase", "Measured in orders").

Library by vertical:

**Ecommerce / DTC retail**
1. *Concierge chat that sells* — "Answers **sizing, stock, shipping and returns in seconds**, then walks the shopper to checkout. One shared email or phone number, and the lead **never goes cold**."
2. *Abandoned carts, recovered in conversation* — "Seven of ten carts are abandoned. Nexor leads with the exact item, **resolves the objection that stopped checkout**, and insists on every channel until the order lands."
3. *Winback for customers who went quiet* — "Customers with no orders in 90, 180 or 365 days get worked **one shopper at a time**: what they bought, in their size, with a reason to return. A conversation, not a blast."

**Premium boutique twist** (same skeleton, founder voice + ticket math):
1. *Concierge chat that sells like {founder} would* — "…It knows every fit note in the catalog, so the shirt fits the first time and **the return never happens**."
2. Add the ticket to the stat: "Seven of ten carts are abandoned, and at a $185 shirt every one matters."
3. *Winback for the man who went quiet* — replenishment framing: "The customer who stocked up in March is due for more… what he bought, in his size, with a reason to return."

**Rentals / travel / long lead**
1. *Quotes that convert* — "**Vehicles, car seats and airport pickup answered in seconds.** The quote is built in chat and saved with the trip date."
2. *Long lead trips, nurtured to pickup* — "A family planning six weeks out does not book today. Nexor **checks in as the trip approaches** and closes when the family is ready."
3. *Winback for past renters* — "Past renters get worked **one driver at a time**: where they rented, what they drove, a reason to come back."

For other verticals, map the slots: services → (missed calls answered,
no shows chased, dormant clients reactivated); real estate → (listing
questions, tour no shows, old inquiries); B2B SaaS → (pricing questions,
stalled trials, churned accounts).

## Mock caption

`The SDK on {domain}` + one sentence: "Drops in like any tag.
{Answers/Quotes} from **your {catalog and policies / fleet, rates and
locations}**, before the doubt becomes a bounce." (Drop the bounce clause for
non retail.)

## Page 2 heads

- j-head: `One customer, one memory, every channel.` (rentals: swap customer → renter, etc.)
- stream-head: `One {customer noun} worked across four channels in {N} days` or
  the story shape: `One family trip, six weeks, four channels.`

## The conversation stream (page 2 story)

Six to eight beats. The fixed skeleton:

1. **Web chat** (late night timestamp like 11:52 PM — makes the 24/7 point
   silently). Reuse the page 1 vignette bubbles verbatim, then a system chip
   states the drop off: `Left at checkout · cart abandoned · email and phone on file`.
2. **Email, next morning 9:00 AM.** Warm, plain, from the persona, first
   person, references the chat, restates the one fact that removes the
   objection, link to the saved cart/quote, "If anything else is holding you
   back, just reply. {Persona}". Subject is possessive and concrete: `Your
   {item} is saved` / `How is the {trip} coming along?` Status chip: `Opened · no reply`.
3. **iMessage/WhatsApp same evening.** One sentence + one bolded question:
   `Anything holding you back?` Status: `Read 6:20 PM · left on read`.
4. **The call.** Card, not bubbles: `Outbound call · answered · 2 min 40 s`.
   The call always surfaces a REAL objection that texting missed (wrong size,
   dates moved) and the agent fixes it live, plus one natural upsell (a belt,
   boosters for the kids). This beat is the proposal's proof of intelligence.
5. **Payment link over text.** `As requested: the {fix} is saved and here is
   your payment link.` + the payment card with the order summary line.
6. **Customer pays and thanks the agent.** Then the dark chip:
   `Order placed · sale counted · follow ups stopped` — always this triplet:
   outcome, measurement, restraint.
7. (Long lead variant) one more beat after booking: trip week reminder, then
   `Same thread, same memory · ready for the next trip`.

Timing: retail chases resolve in 3 days; considered purchases span weeks
(Week 0 / Week 2 / Week 3 / Week 4 / Week 6). Escalation order is fixed:
chat → email → text → call. Never two unanswered beats on the same channel in
a row.

## Page 3 blocks

j-head: `We'll set up and iterate agents just for you.` + proof line with only
claims that are true and durable: `Proven on **the biggest retailers in
LATAM** and operations in **8+ countries**.`

Feature grid, four cards, fixed indexes:
- **Memory** / *A reasoning layer that never forgets* — "…in **one thread per customer**. Never asks twice." (boutique adds: "His size, his patterns, his last order.")
- **Control** / *Your rules, enforced* — "Voice, discount authority, frequency caps, quiet hours. Sensitive cases **hand off to your team**."
- **Integration** / *Plugs into your {Shopify} stack* — name their actual stack when known: "Carts and orders come in through **{Shopify} APIs and webhooks**. Sales and replies **sync back to your {CRM / CDP and CRM / email tools}**."
- **Reporting** / *Measured in orders, not opens* — "Every recovered order is **tied to the conversation that won it**, so you see exactly what Nexor added."

Chase beats (`It chases the answer, and it does not tire`): In chat → If no
order → Until yes or no ("Exhausts every means", "No customer lost to
**silence or a missed channel**") → Sync back. The long lead variant retitles
the strip `Six weeks of follow up, zero effort from your team` and the beats
become: In chat / While they plan / Until yes or no / After booking.

Rollout, four phases, always: **Configure** (voice, catalog, policies and
guardrails, with your team) → **Proof of concept** (**paid, 3 months**, live
on a slice of traffic) → **Prove** (compare sales with and without Nexor) →
**Scale** (full traffic, winback waves by segment).

## Words we use, words we avoid

Use: chase, insist, work (a lead), tire, earn a yes or a no, one shopper at a
time, a conversation not a blast, measured in orders, sale counted, follow ups
stopped, drops in like any tag, before the doubt becomes a bounce.

Avoid: leverage, seamless, cutting edge, revolutionize, AI powered (as a
selling point), best in class, delight, journey (as marketing noun), any
em dash or hyphen in visible copy, exclamation marks outside customer bubbles.
